# Abstract 

Unaligned RGBT tracking aims to achieve robust target localization across spatially misaligned RGB and thermal infrared (TIR) videos, a crucial challenge for deploying RGBT tracking in real-world scenarios. Existing methods often calculate all cross-modal alignment parameters (i.e., spatial shift and scale change) simultaneously, but suffer from two major limitations. 1) They are difficult to adapt to different degrees of unaligned difficulty during tracking. 2) They usually require complex models to handle challenging scenarios, resulting in a large computational burden. To overcome these limitations, we propose a novel Progressive Multi-cue Alignment framework called PMATrack, which disentangles the calculation of cross-modal alignment pa-rameters in a progressive manner and dynamically selects appropriate cues to handle different challenges, thereby en-abling robust and efficient unaligned RGBT tracking. In particular, PMATrack divides the cross-modal alignment parameter estimation into three stages to progressively per-form center offset computation, scale transformation esti-mation, and global refinement. At each stage, we design a difficulty-aware router to adaptively select the appropri-ate alignment expert based on the cross-modal alignment complexity, thereby reducing computational redundancy. In addition, we build a high-quality video benchmark called MUART244 to facilitate the comprehensive evaluation of different unaligned RGBT tracking algorithms. Extensive experiments demonstrate the outstanding performance of PMATrack against existing state-of-the-art methods. The code and dataset will be available at https://github. com/NOP1224/Unaligned_RGBT_Tracking .

# 1. Introduction 

RGBT tracking [10, 11] is a fundamental task in com-puter vision that aims to achieve robust target localiza-tion and tracking in challenging environments by leverag-

It has broad application potential in security surveillance [4, 6], pedestrian monitoring [5], intelligent robotics, and autonomous driving. However, mainstream RGBT datasets [15, 45] are typically captured using in-dependent sensor systems. Due to installation offsets and field-of-view discrepancies, the raw cross-modal images of-ten exhibit significant spatial misalignment. To facilitate al-gorithm development, these datasets usually undergo costly manual alignment processes, leading most existing RGBT trackers [3, 12, 39] to assume perfect spatial correspon-dence between modalities. This reliance on precise align-ment not only results in high data construction and prepro-cessing costs but also weakens the ability of existing meth-ods to handle unaligned multimodal inputs directly in real-world multi-sensor systems, thereby hindering the practical deployment of RGBT tracking. Although a fixed transformation can partially compen-sate for sensor offsets, the cross-modal correspondence dy-namically changes as the target or camera moves, mak-ing static transformations incapable of maintaining accu-rate alignment. This observation suggests that achieving robust multimodal tracking in realistic conditions necessi-tates models that can dynamically predict and correct cross-modal alignment throughout the tracking process. Despite recent efforts [21, 47] to mitigate cross-modal misalignment through feature or geometry-level alignment, they still suf-fer from two fundamental limitations. (1) They estimate all alignment parameters simultaneously, making it difficult to adapt to varying degrees of misalignment during track-ing; (2) Their static alignment architectures typically re-quire complex models to handle challenging scenarios, re-sulting in substantial computational overhead. To address these challenges, we propose PMATrack, which progressively reduces cross-modal spatial misalign-ment through hierarchical bias correction. It further per-forms guided feature fusion from shallow to deep layers, achieving robust geometric and semantic alignment. # 2. Related Work 

## 2.1. RGBT Tracking 

With the rapid advancement of deep learning [18, 19, 31, 46], recent RGBT tracking methods can be grouped into mid-level fusion and late fusion approaches. Mid-level fu-sion emphasizes the integration of complementary informa-tion from intermediate network features. For example, Lu et 35208 al. [27] propose a differential Mamba [8] with linear com-plexity for fusing multimodal information. The late fusion methods focus on weighting multimodal features after fea-ture extraction, based on the quality of each modality. For instance, Liu et al. [20] employ a three-branch network to estimate modality reliability weights as pseudo-labels and use them to guide multimodal fusion. However, these meth-ods rely heavily on the assumption of highly aligned modal-ities, making them unsuitable for real-world scenarios with severe spatial misalignment. Some works have attempted to address the unaligned RGBT tracking problem. For exam-ple, Zhang et al. [47] explicitly predict cross-modal offset fields via deformable convolution and suppress mismatched regions using a mask to mitigate weak misalignment, while Liu et al. [21] employ a temporally iterative homography estimation module for continuous geometric alignment be-tween frames. Nevertheless, these methods estimate all cross-modal alignment parameters simultaneously, making it difficult to adapt to varying levels of misalignment during tracking. To this end, we propose to decouple the estima-tion of cross-modal alignment parameters, enabling robust and efficient unaligned RGBT tracking. 

## 2.2. Cross-Modal Image Alignment 

In recent years, an increasing number of researchers [29, 43] have focused on the misalignment problem between mul-timodal images, aiming to handle real-world data directly and avoid labor-intensive alignment preprocessing. For in-stance, Song et al. [33] adapt the visual foundation model DINOv2 [30] to the medical domain, achieving multi-modal image alignment without additional training. Tang et al. [34] propose a joint optimization framework for multi-modal image alignment and fusion, where the fusion results provide feedback to guide the alignment, thus improving geometric alignment accuracy. Yu et al. [41] decompose un-supervised cross-modal alignment into intra-modal homog-raphy estimation and modality transfer, achieving unsuper-vised yet high-precision multimodal geometric alignment. However, these methods often rely on heavy models that process both simple and complex scenes simultaneously, resulting in excessive computational overhead and making it difficult to meet the real-time requirements of tracking. To address this issue, we propose a difficulty-aware multi-cue expert selection mechanism that dynamically selects the optimal alignment cue based on scene complexity, thereby achieving a balanced trade-off between performance and ef-ficiency. 

# 3. Methodology 

## 3.1. Overview 

This paper presents PMATrack, a progressive alignment framework for unaligned RGBT tracking. As shown in Fig. 3, we decompose cross-modal alignment into three se-quential components: center alignment, scale transforma-tion, and global refinement. This divide-and-conquer de-sign avoids directly regressing a single homography matrix with coupled parameters. At each alignment stage, we em-ploy a difficulty-aware multi-cue expert (DMAE) module that dynamically adjusts the complexity of the alignment network based on the difficulty of the current scene, thereby reducing redundant computation. Specifically, the track-ing backbone first extracts the template and search features. The center alignment parameters are predicted at the shal-low stage, after which the predicted offsets guide bidirec-tional cross-modal fusion before passing features to deeper layers. The scale transformation and global refinement pa-rameters are then estimated progressively, each followed by cross-modal fusion. The enhanced multimodal features are finally concatenated and fed into the tracking head to local-ize the target. The following sections detail each compo-nent. 

## 3.2. Cross-modal Progressive Alignment 

To address the multi-scale spatial offsets in unaligned RGBT scenarios, we design a hierarchical progressive alignment strategy. This strategy performs offset predic-tion at the shallow, middle, and deep layers of the network, achieving coarse-to-fine cross-modal correction. The shal-low features preserve geometry information, enabling the alignment network to accurately predict the center offset by capturing pixel-level correlations between modalities. After passing through multiple attention layers, the middle-level features gradually aggregate global contextual information and are used to estimate scale variation and refine the center offset. Finally, we use the high-level semantic information to globally refine residual misalignments caused by issues such as occlusion or modality discrepancies. ## 3.4. Transformation-guided Cross-Modal Fusion 

Previous multimodal trackers [1, 13] typically rely on well-aligned multimodal information for feature fusion, mak-ing it difficult to achieve effective cross-modal complemen-tarity under severe misalignment. However, directly per-forming feature alignment during fusion often introduces noise [32] (see supplementary materials). To enable ef-fective complementary fusion in misaligned scenarios, we propose a Transformation-guided Cross-Modal Deformable Attention (TCMDA) module based on deformable atten-tion [50]. After each alignment stage, the predicted off-set is converted into a 3 × 3 homography matrix H, which generates an initial sampling grid pt for cross-modal align-ment. Each target point pt is projected onto the source modality by ps = Ht→spt, and their coordinate difference 

∆H = ps − pt provides a geometric prior for sampling. To refine residual errors, local offsets and attention weights are learned from query features via small MLPs, and combined with ∆H to produce the final sampling position: 

Gh,k = pt + ∆ Hh + ∆ Lh,k (7) The source features S are then sampled at Gh, k and ag-gregated with attention weights to obtain fused features

By combining geo-metric priors and learnable local offsets, TCMDA achieves alignment-guided cross-modal fusion, effectively mitigat-ing information loss caused by spatial misalignment.

We then compute the IoU between these two templates on the search region of the initial frame to assess the reliability of the update. # 4. Experiment 

We conduct a comprehensive evaluation of 11 state-of-the-art trackers on the LasHeR-Unaligned [17] and MUART244 unaligned datasets. Three widely used evaluation metrics, including Precision Rate (PR), Success Rate (SR), and Nor-malized Precision Rate (NPR) from the One Pass Evalu-ation (OPE) protocol, are adopted for quantitative perfor-mance assessment, which are standard in current RGBT tracking benchmarks. In addition, we compare the perfor-mance and efficiency of incorporating existing cross-modal alignment methods into the baseline tracker, as detailed in the supplementary material .

## 4.1. Datasets 

We retrain all trackers on the training set of LasHeR-Unaligned [17] and conduct detailed evaluations on both the LasHeR-Unaligned test set and our MUART244 dataset. Our MUART244 dataset is the first multi-platform un-aligned RGBT tracking dataset, featuring precise dual-modal annotations without any spatial cropping or align-ment operations. It includes 26 object categories and 22 challenge types, covering various resolutions ranging from 

1600 × 1200&640 × 480 to 3840 × 2160&1280 × 1024 .This provides a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating unaligned RGBT tracking across heterogeneous platforms. For more details on MUART244, please refer to the supple-mentary material .

## 4.2. Implementation Details 

We implement PMATrack based on PyTorch and train it on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU. The hyperparameter set-tings of the loss functions are kept consistent with those of the baseline model [40]. For parameter initialization, we use the pretrained weights provided by DropMAE [36]. During training, we adopt the AdamW [23] optimizer with a weight decay of 1×10 −4, and set the batch size and learn-ing rate to 16 and 1×10 −4, respectively. The entire network is trained in two stages, each lasting 20 and 30 epochs, with 60,000 sample pairs provided per epoch.

## 4.5. Parameter Analysis 

Effect of Online Offset Prediction. As shown in Ta-ble 4, to verify the necessity of online offset prediction, we conduct experiments on the MUART244 dataset us-ing five SOTA RGBT trackers, where each tracker em-ploys the initial-frame cross-modal offset to align all sub-sequent frames. Most trackers (e.g., SDSTrack, SUTrack, CAFormer) exhibit a clear performance drop, while BAT and UnTrack remain relatively stable or even slightly im-prove. This degradation occurs because MUART244 con-tains more severe and dynamically varying misalignment cases. The offset estimated from the first frame can only provide alignment during the early stages; as the sequence progresses, camera motion or target movement across the image center can cause the offset direction to reverse (see 

supplementary material ), leading to misalignment and thus breaking cross-modal consistency.

We observe that the pro-posed PMATrack consistently outperforms other methods (AINet, CAFormer, SUTrack, and BAT) under most chal-lenges, and achieves particularly notable gains in cases with severe cross-modal misalignment (e.g., RM, VM, and HM). 

Visualization of Progressive Alignment. As shown in Fig. 6, we visualize the progressive alignment process on unaligned RGBT sequences. Comparing the first and sec-ond columns reveals a clear spatial offset between the two modalities. Across the three alignment stages, the model first predicts the center offset to achieve coarse alignment, then applies a scale transformation to adjust the field-of-view discrepancy and refine the remaining misalignment. Finally, a global refinement step further corrects residual errors, resulting in accurate cross-modal alignment. These results demonstrate that the proposed progressive align-ment strategy effectively eliminates spatial discrepancies in a coarse-to-fine manner. 

Visualization of Expert Selection. As illustrated in Fig. 7, we visualize the expert-selection behavior of our model un-der different scenarios. In relatively simple cases, the model predominantly selects the lightweight TRE expert. How-ever, when the target becomes occluded or camera motion induces blur, the model shifts toward the more computa-tionally intensive but accurate experts FME and DPE to ex-ploit richer contextual information and better correct cross-modal misalignment. 

# 5. Conclusion 

This paper presents PMATrack, a framework for cross-modal spatial alignment and feature fusion that addresses the challenges of unaligned RGBT tracking. The frame-work decomposes the alignment parameters into center offset, scale transformation, and global refinement, en-abling a divide-and-conquer strategy for progressively pre-dicting these parameters. At each alignment stage, adifficulty-aware router adaptively selects the most suitable expert from three cue-specific experts to balance align-ment accuracy and computational cost. In addition, we introduce TCMDA to mitigate information loss caused by spatial misalignment, and TOCU to robustly maintain and update dynamic offsets during inference. PMATrack achieves state-of-the-art performance on both the LasHeR-Unaligned dataset and our newly constructed MUART244 dataset, which features multi-platform configurations, un-aligned modalities, heterogeneous resolutions, and more challenging real-world scenarios.
