## Abstract 

Cross-modal alignment aims to learn semantically consis-tent latent representations across diverse modalities. Pre-vailing methods rely on a text-guided aggregation paradigm to achieve fine-grained alignment, while they suffer from re-dundant patch-word correlations and high computational costs. To address these issues, we propose CoV-Align, an effective and efficient fine-grained cross-modal alignment framework with cohesive visual semantics priority. Through a semantically convergent attention mechanism, it progres-sively aggregates meaningful visual patches in a text-free manner. We design a coarse visual semantic feature ex-tractor that integrates deformable attention and consistent assign attention to group patches with semantic consis-tency. A cohesive and discriminative feature optimization is presented to enhance intra-semantic cohesion and inter-semantic discriminability of visual region features, result-ing in explicit improvements in cross-modal alignment. Ex-tensive experiments demonstrate that CoV-Align achieves state-of-the-art performance on the Flickr30K and MS-COCO benchmarks. Notably, it delivers a 3–5 → compu-tational speedup compared to pioneer approaches, offering compelling advantages for large-scale multi-modal tasks. 

## 1. Introduction 

Cross-modal alignment, which aims to bridge the seman-tic gap between diverse modalities, is a key problem in many multi-modal tasks, including image captioning and visual question answering. Existing solutions can be cat-egorized into two main paradigms: coarse-grained align-ment [8, 24] and fine-grained alignment [16, 23]. The for-mer, as depicted in Figure 1 (a), focuses on the global scene and major objects, failing to capture the semantic details [7, 11, 20, 30]. In fine-grained alignment, object detection based approaches were initially adopted [16, 23], but they are constrained by the fixed category divisions of the detector, making them unsuitable for open-domain downstream tasks. Subsequently, Transformer-based meth-ods [7, 30] selectively associate visual patches with corre-sponding text through attention mechanisms, as shown in Figure 1 (b). However, individual visual patches usually lack complete semantic information, resulting in ambiguous word matching [10]. To address this issue, recent meth-ods [1, 10] adopt text-guided cross-attention strategy, as il-lustrated in Figure 1 (c). Resorting to the text embeddings, the visual patches are aggregated at the semantic level, re-porting state-of-the-art alignment performance. However, the text-guided aggregation paradigm com-putes alignment weights between each text query and all visual patches in the image, leading to two problems. 1) Redundant patch-word alignments. It inevitably introduces noise from semantically irrelevant patches, diluting criti-cal semantic information and compromising retrieval ac-curacy. 2) High computational cost. For large-scale re-trieval with millions of images, this repeated aggregation leads to substantial latency and memory consumption, lim-iting practical deployment. If meaningful visual semantics, such as the “hand”, “pushing”, and “cart” regions in an image of “a person pushing a cart”, could be linked in advance with-out the text guidance, other irrelevant visual regions would not need to participate in cross-attention computation, as in Figure 1 (d). In this paper, we propose CoV-Align, an effective and ef-ficient fine-grained cross-modal alignment framework with Cohesive Visual Semantics Priority. Unlike existing meth-ods that introduce text to aggregate visual patches before alignment, CoV-Align originally adopts text-free aggrega-tion paradigm. Precise region generation with a seman-tically convergent attention improves alignment accuracy, while text-independent aggregation reduces computational complexity. First, we design a coarse visual semantic fea-ture extractor that integrates deformable attention and con-sistent assign attention with dynamic mask. This enables coarse grouping of semantically consistent patches indepen-dently without text guidance. Second, we present a cohesive and discriminative feature optimization method to enhance intra-semantic cohesion and inter-semantic discriminability. A spatial concentration loss is designed to leverage attention maps to tighten local concentration of semantically relevant regions, while a visual contrastive loss is tailored to improve the discriminability of visual semantic features. Finally, the optimized region features are fed into a sparse region-word alignment module for the fine-grained cross-modal align-ment. The contributions can be summarized as follows. • We propose a novel CoV-Align framework, which achieves a win-win scenario: precise fine-grained vi-sual region generation improves alignment accuracy, while text-independent aggregation reduces computa-tional complexity. • We design a coarse visual semantic feature extractor to group patches to regions with semantic consistency. A cohesive and discriminative feature optimization is presented to enhance intra-semantic cohesion and inter-semantic discriminability of region features. • CoV-Align achieves state-of-the-art performance on the Flickr30K and MS-COCO benchmarks. Critically, it demonstrates superior efficiency, delivering up to almost a 3–5 → speedup and a significant reduction in FLOPs compared to the top counterparts. 

## 2. Related Work Coarse-grained Alignment Methods. Image-text align-ment aims to measure the semantic similarity between vi-sual and textual data, which is broadly divided into coarse-grained and fine-grained approaches. Coarse-grained meth-ods align the global representations of an entire image and a complete text caption. Early works [3, 8] first extract re-gional features using object detectors [25] and then aggre-gate them into a single global vector. More recent meth-ods, notably CLIP [24], employ an end-to-end dual-encoder architecture to directly learn global embeddings. These approaches simplify the pipeline and have shown strong generalization. However, these global methods struggle to capture the fine-grained correspondences between specific image regions and textual phrases, limiting overall perfor-mance in complex scenes. 

Fine-grained Alignment Methods. Fine-grained meth-ods are developed to address this limitation by focusing on local-level alignment between image regions and words. Initial approaches like SCAN [16] rely on the object de-tectors like Faster-RCNN [25] for region proposals, but are constrained by the detector’s fixed vocabulary and high computational cost. With the great success of Vision Trans-formers, detector-free models, e.g., FILIP [30], compute the similarity across all patch-token pairs for alignment, but are sensitive to noisy or redundant patches. To refine this, sub-sequent works such as LAPS [10] and SPARC [1] introduce cross-modal attention, using textual context to guide the ag-gregation of visual features. While these approaches show promising results, text-guided aggregation significantly in-creases computational complexity and inference latency, making it challenging for large-scale retrieval. In view of this, this paper focuses on extracting high-quality, semanti-cally concentrated region features without textual guidance, achieving both high accuracy and efficiency. 

## 3. Methodology 

As mentioned in Sec. 1, the proposed CoV-Align is able to generate fine-grained visual semantic tokens indepen-dently of text, improving both region-text matching accu-racy and efficiency. The overview of CoV-Align is shown in Figure 2. Given an image-text pair, patch and text fea-tures are extracted via the visual and the text encoder. The coarse-grained visual semantic feature extractor then iden-tifies salient regions, which are refined by the cohesive and discriminative feature optimization. Finally, a sparse align-ment module computes region-word similarity.

3.2. Cohesive and Discriminative Feature Optimiza-tion 

After coarse visual semantic feature extraction, the attention distributions corresponding to region queries often disperse to distant patches, causing the spatial drift issue. Moreover, the partitioned regions tend to exhibit spatial overlaps in the projection view, resulting in ambiguous region representa-tions. To address these limitations, we present a Cohesive and Discriminative Feature Optimization (CDFO) with two complementary constraints: a Spatial Concentration Loss to enforce spatial concentration within each region feature, and a Visual Contrastive Loss to promote discriminability across features of different regions. These constraints col-laboratively optimize the models, enabling region represen-tations to achieve both spatial concentration and semantic discriminability. 

Spatial Concentration Loss Constraint. In vision tasks, the high semantic correlation among neighboring pix-els constitutes a fundamental prior for spatial continuity. In view of this, we design a spatial concentration loss to strengthen the local pattern with definite semantics. We employ ViT-B/16, Swin-B/32, and CLIP-ViT-B/L as visual backbones, paired with BERT or CLIP text en-coders. All features are subsequently projected to a 512-dimensional shared space. The framework is trained for 30 epochs with the Adam optimizer, using a learning rate of 1e-4, a weight decay of 1e-4, and a cosine annealing schedule. The hyperparameters ϑg, ϑv, ϑc, ε and Nq are assigned with 1, 1, 0.5, 0.7 and 8, respectively. The loss coefficients ( ϑg, ϑv, ϑc,) are determined through extensive ablation studies detailed in Appendix A.

## 5. Conclusions 

In this paper, we introduce an effective and efficient fine-grained cross-modal alignment framework with cohesive visual semantics priority, termed CoV-Align, to overcome two major limitations in cross-modal alignment, i.e., redun-dant patch-word correlations and high computational costs. Specifically, CoV-Align achieve efficient fine-grained align-ment by progressively aggregating visual regions without text guidance. Through a Coarse Visual Semantic Feature Extractor as well as a Cohesive and Discriminative Fea-ture Optimization, it enhances intra-semantic cohesion and inter-semantic discriminability of visual region features, contributing to explicit improvements in cross-modal align-ment. Extensive experiments on the Flickr30K and MS-COCO datasets demonstrate the superiority of our frame-work. It has strong potential for downstream tasks in mul-timodal large models owing to its dual advantages in both accuracy and computational efficiency. 36835 6. Acknowledgements
