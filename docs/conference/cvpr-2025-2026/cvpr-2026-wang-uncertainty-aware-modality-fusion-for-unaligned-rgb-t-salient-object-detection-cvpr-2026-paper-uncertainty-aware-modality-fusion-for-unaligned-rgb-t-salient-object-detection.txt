## Abstract 

Unaligned RGB-T salient object detection (SOD) remains challenging due to severe cross-modal spatial discrepan-cies and unreliable feature fusion. Existing methods of-ten assume perfect alignment or rely on geometric reg-istration, which is computationally demanding and sensi-tive to cross-modal inconsistencies. To address these limi-tations, we propose an uncertainty-aware modality fusion network (UMFNet) that reformulates RGB-T SOD as an uncertainty-aware representation learning problem. Specif-ically, the proposed uncertainty alignment module (UAM) models pixel-wise features as Gaussian latent distributions to estimate local uncertainty and identify cross-modal con-sistency regions within the feature space, thereby achiev-ing implicit alignment without explicit registration. Fur-thermore, the confidence-guided global modulation (CGM) mechanism leverages confidence maps derived from un-certainty estimation to adaptively regulate the fusion of RGB and thermal features, enhancing salient cues in re-liable regions while suppressing noisy or inconsistent in-formation. Extensive experiments on five unaligned and three aligned RGB-T SOD benchmarks demonstrate that UMFNet achieves state-of-the-art performance across di-verse alignment conditions.

Such cross-modal misalignment introduces significant discrepancies at the pixel level, leading to feature distortion and semantic inconsistency during feature fusion. These issues increase the risk of erroneous information integration and ultimately degrade detection performance [37]. Motivated by the limi-tations of existing methods under spatial misalignment, we focus on designing RGB-T SOD models capable of cross-modal fusion without relying on explicit registration. To address cross-modal misalignment, most existing methods adopt explicit geometric registration strategies [25, 41]. For example, in RGB-T object detection, affine trans-formation prediction and deformable convolutions have been employed to achieve object-level alignment [18, 40], as shown in Fig.1(b). However, these methods are com-putationally intensive and highly sensitive to parallax, oc-clusion, and modality degradation, which often compro-mises robustness and generalization. To mitigate the re-liance on precise geometric alignment, recent studies have investigated alternative mechanisms such as high-level se-mantic guidance [13], cross-modal attention [8], local win-dow matching [34], and graph-based structural constraints [38]. These methods help alleviate accumulated noise and feature distortions. Nonetheless, in pixel-level tasks such as SOD, especially under semantically ambiguous con-ditions, these methods remain insufficient in addressing cross-modal pixel-wise alignment. Therefore, achieving cross-modal pixel-wise consistency and learning robust aligned thermal representations without relying on ex-plicit geometric registration has become a critical chal-lenge in RGB-T SOD .After obtaining the aligned thermal features, existing methods typically enhance cross-modal complementarity by injecting them into RGB features through attention-based strategies [24]. However, due to the inherent dif-ferences in semantic distributions between RGB and ther-mal modalities, the spatial reliability of the aligned fea-tures remains significantly uneven [32]. If such unreli-able information is ignored during the fusion process, er-roneous information from modality conflicts or low-quality regions may be introduced, leading to fusion bias and degra-dation of the discriminative capacity of the original RGB features [44]. These effects are particularly severe in un-aligned scenes, where the semantic gap between the two modalities tends to widen [4]. In such cases, conventional attention-based strategies not only struggle to identify unre-liable information within the thermal features but also fail to effectively exploit the complementary strengths of RGB and thermal data. Thus, dynamically evaluating the local reliability of aligned thermal features during the fusion stage, while effectively suppressing unreliable noise, has emerged as the second key challenge in RGB-T SOD. 

In this paper, we propose the uncertainty-aware modal-ity fusion network (UMFNet) to address the aforemen-tioned challenges of cross-modal unaligned fusion. In contrast to methods that rely on explicit geometric regis-tration, UMFNet formulates cross-modal alignment as an uncertainty-aware representation learning problem within the feature distribution space. Specifically, we design an uncertainty alignment module (UAM) that models pixel-wise uncertainty by reconstructing modality-specific fea-tures as Gaussian latent distributions. This strategy enables the learning of robust local feature representations within each modality, while leveraging the latent distribution space to capture cross-modal consistency. Furthermore, we intro-duce a confidence-guided global modulation (CGM) mech-anism, where confidence maps derived from uncertainty es-timation serve as gating signals to adaptively regulate the integration of aligned infrared features into RGB represen-tations. This mechanism facilitates globally reliable fusion by attenuating the influence of uncertain regions, thereby suppressing noise and enhancing the stability and accuracy of saliency prediction. Extensive experiments demonstrate that UMFNet achieves state-of-the-art performance on both aligned and unaligned datasets, thus validating both its ef-fectiveness and robustness. Our main contributions are: • We introduce UMFNet that reformulates unaligned RGB-T fusion via feature-space uncertainty modeling. Within UMFNet, the proposed UAM module models pixel-wise features as Gaussian latent distributions, allowing for the discovery of local cross-modal consistency and enabling robust alignment without explicit registration. • To enhance the reliability of cross-modal fusion, we pro-pose the CGM module that leverages pixel-wise confi-dence maps generated by UAM to adaptively regulate RGB and thermal feature fusion, strengthening saliency representation in high-confidence regions while suppress-ing unreliable information in low-confidence regions. • Extensive experiments on five unaligned and three aligned RGB-T saliency detection datasets demonstrate that UAMF effectively handles cross-modal misalign-ment while maintaining strong performance under ideal registration, confirming its robustness and generalization across varying alignment conditions. 

## 2. Related Work 

2.1. RGB-T Salient Object Detection 

RGB-T SOD leverages the complementary properties of visible and thermal infrared modalities to enhance object localization under challenging conditions [27]. By fusing heterogeneous cross-modal cues, these methods offer ro-bust performance against illumination variations and tex-ture deficiencies, facilitating effective perception in night-time and degraded environments [11]. With the advance-41490 ment of deep learning, CNN-based dual-stream architec-tures have become the standard [12, 14, 31], with cross-modal interaction evolving from late fusion to more sophis-ticated intermediate strategies [5]. To further refine saliency representation, attention mechanisms are widely integrated across spatial and channel dimensions [19, 45]. More re-cently, Transformer-based models have been introduced to RGB-T SOD to exploit their superior global dependency modeling [16, 35]. For instance, HRTransNet [23] com-bines attention-based modality injection with dual-path re-finement to achieve high-quality multi-resolution fusion. Despite these developments, most existing methods oper-ate under the assumption of spatially aligned inputs, which constrains their practical applicability. To bridge this gap, our work targets unaligned RGB-T salient object detection and proposes a framework specifically designed to maintain robustness under spatial misalignment. 

2.2. Unaligned Multi-modal Fusion 

In real-world scenarios, such as UAV-based systems, RGB and thermal images often exhibit spatial misalignment due to inherent sensor discrepancies [13, 43]. This misalign-ment hinders effective feature fusion and significantly de-grades detection and segmentation performance. To address this, specialized fusion strategies have been developed for unaligned cross-modal settings [40, 42]. In object detec-tion, Fu et al. [8] mitigate reliance on image-level reg-istration by dynamically aggregating keypoints in the fea-ture space via deformable attention. Within the context of salient object detection, Lyu et al. [18] integrate con-trastive learning with frequency-domain modeling to en-hance alignment using FFT-based operations. Wang et al. [34] employ asymmetric window partitioning to establish correspondences, leveraging deformable convolutions for fine-grained refinement. Building upon this, PCNet [36] introduces a semantic-guided alignment scheme that pri-oritizes target regions to effectively suppress background noise. Despite these advances, most existing methods rely on deep learning-based geometric registration, which often lacks robustness and generalizability in complex scenarios. To overcome these limitations, we reformulate unaligned RGB-T SOD as a problem of uncertainty-aware representa-tion learning in the feature space. 

## 3. The Proposed Method 

3.1. Overview 

To address the unaligned RGB-T SOD, we propose the uncertainty-aware modality fusion network (UMFNet). As illustrated in Fig.2, UMFNet takes RGB and thermal in-frared images as input and extracts multi-scale features us-ing two parallel Swin Transformer backbones. In the cross-modal alignment stage, we propose the uncertainty align-ment module (UAM) to address structural discrepancies be-tween the visible and thermal modalities. UAM projects the original visible and thermal features into latent Gaus-sian representations with pixel-wise uncertainty, enabling continuous spatial modeling within each modality through reparameterization-based sampling. This probabilistic rep-resentation facilitates the discovery of semantically consis-tent regions across modalities without relying on explicit registration, resulting in aligned thermal features. Building on this, we further introduce the confidence-guided global modulation (CGM) module, which leverages the variance estimates from UAM to generate a confidence map. This map guides the fusion of visible and aligned thermal fea-tures through both spatial and channel-wise modulation, ef-fectively enhancing the adaptability of feature fusion. Fi-nally, the fused multi-scale features are progressively up-sampled in the decoder, and saliency and boundary maps are predicted in parallel at multiple resolutions.

3.4. Loss Function 

To achieve joint optimization of structural modeling, cross-modal alignment and semantic fusion in RGB-T salient ob-ject detection, we construct a unified multi-task loss frame-work. This framework consists of three main objectives in-cluding saliency supervision, boundary modeling and latent distribution regularization. In the saliency branch, binary cross-entropy loss is applied to the four multi-scale predic-tions from the decoder and the final fused output. In the boundary branch, considering the frequent edge ambiguity in real-world scenarios, we adopt a combination of stan-dard BCE loss [21], Dice loss and a tolerant Dice loss [31]. The tolerant Dice loss applies max pooling to the ground-truth boundary map to relax strict pixel-wise alignment con-straints. In the regularization branch, we incorporate the regularization from the UAM module into the overall opti-mization objective to enforce the stability of the latent dis-tributions and suppress over-activation in low-confidence regions. ## 4. Experiment 

4.1. Implementation Details 

Datasets. We evaluate our method on both unaligned and aligned RGB-T datasets. UVT20K [36] is a large-scale un-aligned dataset containing 20,000 training pairs and 20,000 test pairs. Additional unaligned test sets include UVT2000 [34], un-VT821 [28], un-VT1000 [28], and un-VT5000 [28]. For aligned data, VT5000 [29] provides training and test splits, and VT821 [30] and VT1000 [26] are test sets. 

Metrics. We employ three widely used metrics, including S-measure [6], E-measure [7], and weighted F-measure [1]. 

Training and Optimization. The proposed network is implemented using PyTorch and trained on 4 NVIDIA GTX3090 GPUs. The backbone is initialized with the Swin-B pre-trained model [15], while the remaining param-eters are randomly initialized. All input images are resized to 384 × 384 . The Adam optimizer is adopted with a batch size of 64 and an initial learning rate of 5 × 10 −5, which is decreased by a factor of 10 every 100 epochs. 4.2. Comparison with the State-of-the-Art Methods 

To demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method, we compare it with 12 state-of-the-art RGB-T SOD ap-proaches, including MIDD [27], CGFNet [31], SwinNet [16], TNet [5], DCNet [28], HRTransNet [23], MCFNet [19], LSNet [45], CAVER [20], LAFB [35], SACNet [34], and PCNet [36]. Notably, DCNet, SACNet, and PCNet are specifically designed for unaligned RGB-T SOD. All results are obtained either from the original papers or by reproduc-ing the authors’ released code with default parameters. 

Quantitative Evaluation. Table 1 presents the quantita-tive comparison results of the proposed UMFNet on two un-aligned datasets UVT20K and UVT2000 and three weakly aligned datasets un-VT5000, un-VT1000, and un-VT821. Compared with thirteen state-of-the-art methods, UMFNet achieves the best performance across all five datasets in three key evaluation metrics Sα, ES , and F wβ , comprehen-sively demonstrating its strong adaptability and stability un-der various scenarios and alignment deviations. Particularly on the most challenging unaligned dataset UVT20K, UMFNet achieves Sα, ES , and F wβ scores of 0.890, 0.918, and 0.836, respectively, surpassing the sub-optimal method PCNet by 1.9%, 0.7%, and 2.8%. This demonstrates its remarkable robustness under conditions lacking spatial consistency. Similarly, on the weakly aligned datasets un-VT5000 and un-VT1000, UMFNet at-tains F wβ scores of 0.882 and 0.925, showing significant im-provements of 8.3% and 5.1% over SACNet, further verify-ing its superior generalization ability in handling structural misalignment and visual shift scenarios. Table 2 further presents the performance of UMFNet on three standard aligned datasets VT5000, VT1000, and VT821. The results indicate that UMFNet achieves over-all superior performance compared with existing compet-ing methods. In summary, whether on unaligned, weakly aligned, or standard aligned datasets, UMFNet demon-strates highly stable performance superiority.

## 5. Conclusion 

This paper introduces a novel UMFNet for unaligned RGB-T SOD that addresses the challenges of cross-modal spatial misalignment and unreliable feature fusion. Instead of re-lying on explicit geometric registration, UMFNet reformu-lates the alignment problem as uncertainty-aware represen-tation learning within a probabilistic feature space, enabling more robust and adaptive fusion across modalities. The pro-posed UAM models local pixel-wise features as Gaussian latent distributions to estimate cross-modal consistency and mitigate feature distortion caused by misalignment. In ad-dition, the CGM mechanism employs confidence maps de-rived from uncertainty estimation to adaptively regulate in-frared–RGB fusion, enhancing salient representations in re-liable regions while suppressing noise in uncertain areas. Therefore, UMFNet achieves alignment-free cross-modal consistency and adaptive fusion, delivering accurate and stable saliency detection with strong robustness and gen-eralization on both aligned and unaligned RGB-T datasets.
