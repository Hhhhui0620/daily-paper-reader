Abstract 

Cross-degradation generalization remains a critical chal-lenge for RGB-infrared multimodal object detection, espe-cially when training data covers limited degradation types. This paper presents a distribution alignment framework with a key insight: aligning fused features to the pre-trained distribution where the frozen detector performs op-timally, rather than adapting to training-specific degrada-tions. By freezing the pretrained detector and training only a lightweight fusion module, our approach leverages com-plementary infrared information to reduce distribution shift while maintaining computational efficiency. The method achieves state-of-the-art results on three benchmarks with 4× faster training. Critically, we demonstrate that align-ing to the pretrained distribution substantially outperforms aligning to training degradations when generalizing to un-seen scenarios. 

1. Introduction 

Robust object detection under diverse environmental con-ditions is crucial for safety-critical applications such as au-tonomous driving and surveillance systems. RGB cameras, serving as the primary sensing modality, suffer from se-vere performance degradation under challenging illumina-tion conditions including overexposure, underexposure, and nighttime scenarios [37]. Infrared cameras provide com-plementary information that is less sensitive to illumina-tion variations [14, 23, 35], making RGB-IR multimodal detection a promising solution for all-weather perception. However, existing fusion methods still face significant chal-lenges in extreme scenarios where RGB features deviate substantially from the pretrained distribution. Recent multimodal fusion methods employ attention-based [10, 12, 29] and advanced cross-modal interaction de-signs [6, 9, 16, 18, 19, 22, 27, 30, 32, 33]. However, a crit-

2. Related Work 

Multimodal Fusion and Parameter-Efficient Methods 

Multimodal fusion combines information from different modalities for robust perception [1, 14]. Recent methods employ attention-based [10, 12, 29] and advanced cross-modal interaction designs [6, 9, 16, 17, 19, 20, 22, 25, 32, 34] fusion strategies via end-to-end training. Advanced fu-sion designs [7, 15, 36] introduce lightweight modules to enhance cross-modal interaction, but rely on task loss alone for implicit feature guidance. We explicitly model the pre-trained distribution via GMM and use alignment loss for direct distribution supervision, providing stronger guidance for cross-degradation generalization. 

Domain Adaptation and Feature Space Learning Do-main adaptation addresses distribution shift through feature alignment [2, 4, 8, 13, 21, 28]. Theory suggests that fea-tures learned from diverse source data provide better gen-eralization than task-specific adaptation [2]. We leverage this insight: rather than adapting to target degradations, we use multimodal complementarity to reduce distribution shift while preserving the pretrained distribution, enabling better generalization to unseen degradations. 

3. Methodology 

We introduce a distribution alignment framework for mul-timodal object detection that transforms degraded features back toward the pretrained feature distribution. As illus-trated in Figure 2, our method consists of two stages: (1) Of-fline : model the pretrained distribution Pnormal from normal scenarios, which serves as our alignment target; (2) Online :freeze the detector and train a lightweight fusion module with explicit alignment to Pnormal . We start by formulating the distribution shift problem and alignment target selection in Sec 3.1, followed by our fusion module architecture in Sec 3.2, and finally the training strategy in Sec 3.3.

For each batch: (1) extract frozen features Frgb , F ir ∈ RN ×d, (2) compute fused fea-tures Frect = M(Frgb , F ir ), (3) compute alignment loss 25497 − log pnormal (F cls rect ) and detection loss Ldet (H(Frect ), Y ), (4) update θM via backpropagation. Freezing decouples fusion learning from detector adaptation, preserving pretrained knowledge. 

4. Experiments 

We validate our method through comprehensive experi-ments that evaluate: (1) overall performance compared to state-of-the-art methods, (2) the impact of alignment target choice on cross-degradation generalization, and (3) the con-tribution of each architectural component. 

4.1. Experimental Setup 

We evaluate on three RGB-IR benchmarks: LLVIP [11] (15,488 pairs, pedestrian), FLIR [26] (10,228 pairs, ve-hicle), and DroneVehicle [24] (28,439 pairs, aerial). We report mAP at IoU=0.5 and separately evaluate normal vs. challenging scenarios (overexposure, underexposure, nighttime). We use frozen ViT-Base [5] (86M params) with DETR [3] head. The fusion module (13M params) employs 8-head cross-attention. We model Pnormal of-fline via GMM ( K = 8) fitted on 5k normal images per dataset. Training uses AdamW (lr= 10 −4, wd= 10 −4), 30 epochs, batch size 8, and λ = 0.5. All experi-ments are conducted on a workstation with AMD EPYC 7532 CPU, NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU (24GB), and 2TB SSD. We compare against attention-based (CBAM [29], SENet [10], MMTM [12]), SOTA end-to-end (ICAFu-sion [22], C2Former [32], M2FNet [19], CFMW [16]), and advanced fusion methods (CrossFormer [15], RSDet [36]). For fair comparison, all methods are instantiated on the same ViT-Base + DETR detector in our implementation. The parameter counts and training times reported in Table 1 are measured under this unified setting. We report mean over 3 runs.

5. Discussion 

Our method shares the goal of efficient multimodal inter-action with advanced fusion approaches [7, 15, 36], and it explicitly models Pnormal via GMM with alignment loss for direct supervision. This design improves generalization to unseen degradations while enabling interpretability through distribution visualization. The trade-off is a one-time GMM fitting cost (approximately 10 minutes), which is negligible compared to training time savings. 

Limitations. The method relies on pretrained detector quality and assumes GMM can approximate feature dis-tributions. When all modalities are severely degraded si-multaneously, complementary information is limited. The approach remains modular, so these limitations can be ad-dressed with stronger pretrained detectors or alternative dis-tribution models without redesigning the fusion architec-ture. 

6. Conclusion 

We present an effective approach for multimodal object de-tection that addresses cross-degradation generalization. Our key insight is that the alignment target matters: explicitly aligning to the pretrained distribution enables superior gen-eralization compared to adapting to training-specific degra-dations. Our method achieves state-of-the-art results on three benchmarks while reducing training time from 14-17 hours to 3.5 hours by freezing the pretrained detector and learning only a lightweight fusion module.
