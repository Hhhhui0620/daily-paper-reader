## Abstract 

Multimodal tiny object detection plays a critical role in real-world applications, yet remains highly challenging due to weak target representations and complex cross-modal in-terference. Existing frequency-domain methods for tiny ob-ject detection are still largely limited to the visible modality and overlook complementary cross-modal frequency cues in multimodal scenes. In this paper, we investigate cross-modal frequency learning for RGBT tiny object detection. Through frequency characteristic analysis, we find that tiny objects in both RGB and infrared modalities contain richer mid- and high-frequency components as object size de-creases. Motivated by this observation, we propose a Dy-namic Frequency-Decoupled Cross-Modal Learning Trans-former (DyFCLT). Specifically, DyFCLT introduces a Dy-namic Frequency-Band Decoupled Cross-Modal Attention (DFCA) mechanism to perform fine-grained cross-modal interaction across dynamic frequency sub-bands, and a Se-lective Smoothing Enhancement (SSE) module to suppress background noise and enhance foreground responses dur-ing multi-scale fusion. Extensive experiments on two RGBT tiny object detection benchmarks and one general-scale benchmark demonstrate that DyFCLT achieves state-of-the-art performance with strong generalization across different scales and scenes.

To fill this gap, we conduct a frequency characteristic analysis on RGBT-Tiny dataset [47]. Specifically, we em-ploy a radial frequency decomposition to divide the normal-ized spectrum into low-, mid-, and high-frequency bands [34], and compute the proportion of the normalized fre-quency energy within each band for RGB and IR modal-ities across different object scales, as shown in Fig. 1(a). The results show that as object size decreases, the propor-tion of mid- and high-frequency energy increases in both modalities. Surprisingly, although the IR modality is gener-ally dominated by low-frequency components, tiny objects still contain abundant frequency information across multi-ple spectral bands. This observation motivates us to focus on fully mining cross-modal frequency information across different spectral bands that is beneficial for tiny objects. However, exploring finer-grained frequency information also carries the risk of introducing more noise from com-plex environments. As illustrated in Fig. 1(b), the vehicles in both modalities are obviously occluded, indicating that directly enhancing the frequency responses in these regions may simultaneously amplify noise and thus harm detection performance. This suggests that extracting target signals requires suppressing complex noise interference while cap-turing rich cross-modal frequency information. To this end, we propose DyFCLT , a Dy namic 

Frequency-Decoupled Cross-modal Learning Tranformer that introduces a new frequency-domain perspective for RGBT TOD. Specifically, we propose a Dy-namic Frequency-Band Decoupled Cross-Modal Attention (DFCA) mechanism to explore fine-grained complementary frequency representations across modalities. Specifically, DFCA decomposes the multimodal features into multiple dynamic sub-bands through radial frequency decomposi-tion, and performs cross-modal interactions and modulation within each band. The band-wise features are then fused to produce cross-modal representations enriched with di-verse and informative frequency cues. To further suppress noise interference and enhance foreground responses, we introduce a Selective Smoothing Enhancement (SSE) mod-ule. SSE predicts a binary mask and make use of channel compression to smooth irrelevant background noise. More-over, to improve multi-scale feature fusion, the background-smoothed features are used to generate a set of sampling kernels that guide the upsampling of low-resolution fea-tures, enhancing foreground-relevant signals within fused representations. DFCA and SSE operate in a synergistic manner, jointly accomplishing a cross-modal feature en-richment–refinement process. Extensive experiments con-ducted on two RGBT TOD benchmarks and one general-size benchmark demonstrate that our approach achieves new state-of-the-art results, surpassing the previous best methods by a significant margin and exhibiting strong gen-eralization across different scales and scenes. 

## 2. Related Work 

2.1. General Small Object Detection 

Existing general small object detection methods fall mainly into the following three categories:(i) Data augmentation: Various augmentation strategies increase the number of training samples, encouraging detectors to focus on in-stances at specific scales during training [15, 16, 67]. (ii) Feature learning: Multi-scale learning methods based on feature pyramids [21], and their variants deeply fuse fea-tures across different levels, significantly enhancing the detection of tiny objects [24, 37]. In addition, recent methods introduce auxiliary self-reconstruction branches to strengthen weak feature representations of tiny objects [4, 45]. Meanwhile, since tiny objects exhibit distinctive frequency characteristics, frequency-domain enhancement is gradually emerging as an effective direction [32, 35]. (iii) Label assignment strategies: Intersection over Union (IoU) is commonly used to match predicted boxes with ground-truth boxes, but it is highly sensitive to positional deviations for tiny objects, which severely degrades label assignment quality in anchor-based detectors. To address this issue, several alternative strategies have been proposed, such as Dot Distance [42], Normalized Gaussian Wasserstein Dis-tance [39], and Receptive Field Label Assignment [43]. Despite these advances, most studies on tiny object de-tection remain limited to the visible-light modality and therefore struggle to handle real-world challenges such as illumination variation and occlusion. This limitation makes multimodal exploration essential for improving the robust-ness of tiny object detection. 

2.2. Visible-Infrared Object Detection 

With the growing exploration of cross-modal feature com-plementarity, feature-level fusion has become the dominant paradigm for RGBT object detection. Early efforts mainly focused on dynamically weighted fusion based on cues such as illumination [36, 59, 63]. With the rise of attention mech-anisms, various attention-based interaction modules have been introduced into cross-modal settings [12, 31, 41, 48]. In parallel, a number of methods have attempted to ex-tract or fuse complementary information in the frequency domain [19, 60, 64]. More recently, preliminary progress has also been made in multimodal detection for tiny-object scenarios. QFDet [56] designs a label assignment strategy for tiny objects by combining prior and posterior knowl-edge. ## 3. Method 

The overall architecture of our proposed method is pre-sented in Fig. 2(a). It consists of three main components: modality-specific feature extractors, dynamic frequency-decoupled cross-modal learning Transformer (DyFCLT) with two branches, and a Transformer-based decoder with deformable attention and a detection head [65]. Given a pair of visible and infrared images Ivis and Iir , the modality-specific feature extractors ϕvis and ϕir extract multi-scale visible features {F lvis }Ll=1 = ϕvis (Ivis ) and infrared fea-tures {F lir }Ll=1 = ϕir (Iir ), where L denotes the number of layers. Then, DyFCLT fuses the multi-scale features from both modalities to generate enhanced representations, by two collaborative components Dynamic Frequency-Band Decoupled Cross-Modal Attention (DFCA) and Selective Smoothing Enhancement (SSE). Subsequently, the multi-scale features from the two modality branches are fused at their corresponding levels, followed by concatenation and flattening. Finally, the decoder cope with flattened to-kens and the head outputs detection results. Unless other-wise specified, all subsequent descriptions take the infrared 

branch as an example, while the same operations are applied to the visible branch.

## 4. Experiment 

4.1. Datasets and Evaluation metrics 

RGBT-Tiny . This dataset [47] is a large-scale benchmark designed for tiny object detection in visible–infrared im-ages, primarily captured from UAV-based remote sensing. It consists of 93k frames and 1.2M manually annotated ob-jects across seven categories and eight diverse scene types. Over 81% of the objects are smaller than 16 × 16 pixels. 

RGBTDronePerson . This dataset [57] is a large-scale benchmark for visible–infrared tiny person detection. It contains 6,125 RGBT image pairs and 70,880 annotated instances across four types of person categories. Notably, 98% of the objects are smaller than 20 pixels, making the dataset highly challenging. 

FLIR . This dataset [49] offers a highly challenging vis-ible–infrared object detection benchmark. In this study, we utilized the “aligned” version, which contains 4,129 pairs for training and 1,013 pairs for testing. It is worth noting that, unlike the previous two datasets, FLIR is not specifi-cally designed for tiny objects and contains a large number of instances at regular scales. 

Evaluation metrics . We adopt AP, AR, and their vari-ants following the standard COCO protocols. Note that tiny object definitions differ between RGBT-Tiny and RGBT-DronePerson. In addition, RGBT-Tiny employs a COCO evaluation metric specifically designed for tiny objects. In our experiments, we adhere to each dataset’s respective def-initions and evaluation settings to ensure fairness. 

4.2. Implementation Details 

We employ pretrained ResNet50 [11] as the backbone for both the visible and infrared branches, and the number of feature layers L is set to 3. The number of B = 3 in DFCA is set to 3. As for data augmentation, we employ only ba-sic ones such as random resize, crop, and flip. For IBS in SSE, the mask supervision is optimized using focal tver-sky loss [1], which is better suited than the standard focal loss for highly imbalanced foreground-background segmen-tation. The training epochs are set to 20 for RGBT-Tiny and RGBTDronePerson, and 50 for FLIR. The learning rate is set to 0.00025 for all three datasets. This demon-strates that DFCA can fully exploit the rich cross-modal in-formation within different frequency bands, thereby bene-fiting tiny object detection.
