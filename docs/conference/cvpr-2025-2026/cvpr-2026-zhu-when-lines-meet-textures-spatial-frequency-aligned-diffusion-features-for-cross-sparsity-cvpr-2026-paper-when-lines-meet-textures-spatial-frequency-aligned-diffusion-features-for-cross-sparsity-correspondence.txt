# When Lines Meet Textures: Spatial-Frequency Aligned Diffusion Features for Cross-Sparsity Correspondence 

## Mingrui Zhu Fengzhi Wang Xin Wei ∗ Jun Wang Nannan Wang ∗ Xinbo Gao Xidian University 

## Abstract 

Establishing accurate correspondence between sparse line representations and rich textured imagery remains a formidable challenge. While diffusion features excel in se-mantic correspondence, they struggle to bridge the fun-damental gap between abstract sketches and texture-rich photographs. We identify two critical disparities: spa-tial domain misalignment from structural abstraction dif-ferences, and frequency domain inconsistencies from tex-ture density variations. Based on this analysis, we propose 

SFA-DIFT , a novel approach that learns spatial-frequency aligned diffusion features for robust cross-modal corre-spondence. Unlike previous methods focusing solely on spa-tial alignment, our key innovation performs dual-domain alignment by learning unified clean diffusion features while strategically aggregating low-frequency components in the frequency domain. This comprehensive spatial-frequency alignment enables equitable understanding between sparse abstractions and rich textures. To validate our approach, we extend the existing sketch-photo correspondence dataset (PSC6K) by generating multi-style textured imagery, cre-ating MS-PSC6K, a comprehensive correspondence bench-mark. Extensive experiments demonstrate that SFA-DIFT achieves state-of-the-art performance, delivering substan-tial improvements with an average of 0.87% on PCK@1, 

2.20% on PCK@5, and 0.95% on PCK@10 over previous best methods, validating the effectiveness and robustness of our dual-domain alignment approach. Codes are available on https://github.com/Mofr77/SFA-DIFT.

Although Stable Diffu-sion (SD) models [24] show promise [31, 36], its features remain heavily biased toward texture and appearance, lead-ing to pronounced distribution shift on pure line drawings. Systematic analyses [11, 15] of these fundamental cross-modal disparities remain limited. Therefore, bridging this representational gap and aligning cross-modal semantics constitute the core challenge of this work. Our analysis reveals two key challenges that limit dif-fusion features for accurate correspondence between sparse line drawings and texture-rich images. First, the standard forward noising process introduces noise, degrading feature purity, which is particularly detrimental to clean line repre-sentations. CleanDIFT [30] addresses this issue by align-ing features across timesteps, allowing for direct feature extraction from clean images. Nevertheless, its effective-ness on sketches remains limited. Second, diffusion mod-els struggle to capture low-frequency semantic information from sketches. While prior work [15] utilized CLIP [23] to modulate low-frequency semantic signals, the method is insufficient to fully bridge the modality gap for establishing dense correspondences. Motivated by these observations, we propose SFA-DIFT , a novel approach that learns spatial-frequency aligned diffusion features for correspondence between sparse line representations and rich textured imagery. Un-like previous methods that exclusively focus on spatial do-main feature alignment, our method addresses the funda-mental disparities between sparse line and rich textured im-agery across both spatial and frequency domains through a dual-domain alignment strategy. To address the spa-tial misalignment of lines, our approach builds upon a pre-trained CleanDIFT backbone and employs parameter-efficient Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) [8] to enhance sketch comprehension through unsupervised fine-tuning on sketch data. This process learns unified clean diffusion features (Unified CleanDIFT) that map both modalities into a shared semantic space while suppressing modality-specific noise. This guides the model to focus on the core geometric structure, making the representation robust to the visual differences between sparse lines and dense textures. To achieve comprehensive spatial-frequency alignment, we further introduce a low-frequency feature aggregation (LoFFA) module that strategically processes low-frequency components. Specifically, the module decomposes multi-scale features via Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) and selectively applies convolution and aggregation to the low-frequency components (LL sub-band), directing the model to focus on shared structural patterns that are crucial for cross-modal correspondence. This dual-domain alignment approach enables our method to bridge both the spatial do-main gaps caused by visual appearance differences and fre-quency domain disparities arising from sketch sparsity. As visualized in Fig. 1(b), the t-SNE [20] visualization reveals that our method achieves superior cross-modal clustering by semantic classes rather than modality types, effectively mapping sparse line drawings and texture-rich images into a unified semantic space, while SD shows high modality dis-crimination with distinct modality-specific clustering. Main contributions are as follows: • We conduct a systematic analysis revealing funda-mental discrepancies between sparse line drawings and texture-rich images across both spatial and frequency domains , and demonstrate that existing diffusion features fail to bridge these multi-domain gaps, resulting in inac-curate correspondence estimation. • We propose SFA-DIFT, which integrates unified clean diffusion features via parameter-efficient LoRA fine-tuning and low-frequency feature aggregation , achiev-ing spatial–frequency alignment for robust correspon-dence estimation between sparse lines and rich textures. • We introduce MS-PSC6K, an extended multi-style ver-sion of PSC6K , providing a comprehensive evaluation protocol for cross-modal correspondence. Extensive ex-periments on both PSC6K and MS-PSC6K demonstrate that our approach achieves state-of-the-art performance, with substantial improvements over previous methods

## 2. Understanding Line-Texture Disparities 

We decompose the fundamental correspondence challenge between sparse line drawings and texture-rich images into two critical perspectives: spatial disparities arising from line drawing abstraction, and frequency disparities stem-ming from texture density variations. Through both con-ceptual analysis and formal characterization, we show how these domain-specific gaps manifest in cross-sparsity cor-respondence tasks. We then critically examine the limita-tions of existing diffusion-based methods in bridging these multi-faceted disparities, establishing the foundation for our dual-domain alignment approach. 

2.1. Spatial Perspective 

The first fundamental gap stems from line drawings be-ing sparse spatial abstractions of object structure. Unlike photographs that capture dense surface detail, sketches are cognitively selective representations [1] that preserve only principal contours while omitting textures and fine geomet-ric features. Effec-tive correspondence thus requires higher-level understand-ing [10] of shared structural topology. 

2.2. Frequency Perspective 

The second gap emerges from fundamental frequency-domain disparities between texture-rich images and sparse line drawings. We quantify this difference through log-magnitude Fourier spectrum analysis (Fig. 2(a, e, i)). Tex-tured images exhibit energy broadly distributed across fre-quencies, with low frequencies capturing macro shape, mid frequencies encoding surface patterns, and high frequen-cies preserving fine details [25]. Conversely, sketches dis-play distinctly different spectral characteristics: dominant low-frequency plateaus from overall structure, broadband high-frequency spikes from sharp edges, and notably sparse mid-frequency content [7]. This spectral mismatch creates three critical challenges: cross-modal feature alignment be-comes problematic in frequency space, texture-dependent representations fail on sketch inputs, and correspondence ambiguity is amplified across domains. 

2.3. Limitations of Prior Efforts 

Early hand-crafted descriptors like SIFT [16] and HOG [18] lack semantic robustness for extreme representation gaps. Recent deep models [4, 12] and advanced correlation op-erators [21] achieve better performance on appearance-consistent pairs, but struggle when modalities exhibit fun-damental differences. The core issue lies in training bias: general models trained on texture-rich images develop in-herent preferences for photometric features. Our PCA anal-ysis of SD features [24] (Fig. 2(b, f, j)) confirms this limi-tation: when processing sparse sketches, SD produces pro-nounced “feature voids” and noise-like artifacts in feature maps, disrupting semantic localization and creating dis-persed, entangled representations. This occurs because the model searches for textures in line-only inputs, causing spatial correspondence collapse. The t-SNE visualization (Fig. 1(b) left) further demonstrates this bias through dis-tinct modality-specific clustering, indicating high modality discrimination that hinders cross-modal understanding. SketchFusion [15] attempts to bridge modality differ-ences by modulating signals with CLIP [23]. While this approach injects high-level semantics and reduces spatial discrepancies by aligning sketch and photo features seman-tically, it addresses symptoms rather than root causes. The method functions as a high-level patch that cannot resolve fundamental low-level frequency mismatches, leaving fea-ture embeddings largely separated (Fig. 2(c, g, k)). CleanDIFT [30] addresses SD’s limitations through de-noising, achieving markedly improved feature smoothness and noise suppression (Fig. 2(d, h, l)). For sketch con-tours [2], CleanDIFT demonstrates “feature densification,” effectively inferring missing spatial structures from sparse line drawings, a clear improvement over SD’s feature dis-continuity. This enhancement manifests as strengthened low-frequency components in both image and sketch fea-tures, visible as brighter, broader central regions in fre-quency spectra, reflecting preference for global shape in-formation while suppressing high-frequency noise. How-ever, this denoising mechanism introduces a critical trade-off: overly smoothed feature maps attenuate semantic ex-pressiveness, particularly for sketches. ## 3. Method 

Based on the dual-domain gap analysis in Sec. 2, we propose Spatial-Frequency Aligned Diffusion Features (SFA-DIFT) to establish effective correspondence between sparse lines and texture-rich imagery. Our approach ad-dresses both gaps through two complementary stages: (1) 

Unified Clean Diffusion Feature Learning bridges spa-tial domain disparities via parameter-efficient fine-tuning, and (2) Low-Frequency Feature Aggregation resolves frequency domain mismatches through wavelet-based pro-cessing. Fig. 3 illustrates the complete framework. 

3.1. Unified Clean Diffusion Feature Learning 

Our analysis demonstrates that existing diffusion features exhibit significant cross-modal misalignment, with sparse sketches and texture-rich images forming separate clusters in the feature space. To address this fundamental limita-tion, we introduce a unified clean diffusion feature (Unified CleanDIFT) learning stage that maps both modalities into a shared semantic subspace while preserving their essen-tial structural information. We employ Low-Rank Adapta-tion (LoRA) [8] to efficiently fine-tune the pre-trained Cle-anDIFT model on sketch data in an unsupervised manner. By integrating LoRA into all linear projection layers of the U-Net backbone, this approach preserves the model’s rich understanding of textured images while effectively adapt-ing to sketch-specific characteristics. 3.2. Low-Frequency Feature Aggregation 

Despite addressing spatial misalignment, a frequency dis-parity persists between low-frequency sketches and broad-spectrum images [11, 37]. To address this, we introduce the Low-Frequency Feature Aggregation (LoFFA) module that enhances shared low-frequency information while sup-pressing modality-specific high-frequency noise. 3.3. Bridging the Spatial and Frequency Gap 

Our two-stage approach achieves effective spatial-frequency alignment. As shown in Fig. 4, Unified CleanDIFT first establishes consistent spatial features across modalities. LoFFA then aggregates shared low-frequency components while suppressing modality-specific high-frequency noise, resulting in concentrated spectral energy in the low-to-mid frequency range and sharpened spatial boundaries. This dual-domain alignment enables robust correspondence between sparse sketches and texture-rich images.

4.4. Ablation Study 

We evaluate component contributions using averaged met-rics across MS-PSC6K and PSC6K (Tab. 4). Zero-shot pre-trained models like CleanDIFT show limited effectiveness, highlighting the inadequacy of general-purpose features for fine-grained cross-modal matching. To address this, we introduce the LoFFA module and val-idate its design through five ablation settings. Compared to a fairly supervised Unified CleanDIFT + Conv ‡ base-line, LoFFA yields significant gains. Internally, variants including LoFFA without AdaIN ‡, LoFE to Conv ‡, DWT and IDWT to Conv ‡, and Single DWT and IDWT ‡ con-firm the necessity of distribution alignment, low-frequency enhancement, explicit frequency transforms, and deep fre-quency decomposition, respectively. SFA-DIFT outper-forms all variants, proving frequency-aware alignment ef-fectively bridges the modality gap. 

## 5. Conclusion 

This paper addresses the challenging correspondence prob-lem between sparse line drawings and texture-rich pho-tographs by identifying fundamental cross-modal discrep-ancies. We reveal that correspondence failures stem from spatial abstraction gaps and frequency distribution incon-sistencies. Our SFA-DIFT framework tackles these chal-lenges through parameter-efficient LoRA-based fine-tuning for spatial alignment and lightweight low-frequency aggre-gation for frequency reconciliation. Extensive evaluation on our MS-PSC6K benchmark demonstrates state-of-the-art performance with significant improvements. The re-sults validate that effective cross-sparsity correspondence requires simultaneous spatial and frequency domain align-ment, providing a principled framework for understanding and addressing line-texture mismatches. 

Limitation and Future Work. The use of Diffusion fea-tures in our approach presents inherent limitations in in-ference efficiency, with an average processing time of 0.8 seconds per correspondence estimation.
