# Abstract 

Mitigating noisy correspondence in cross-modal match-ing poses a serious challenge due to the problem of error ac-cumulation. Existing methods primarily attribute this accu-mulation to errors caused by noisy sample pairs. However, a novel source of error from clean sample pairs (also termed anchor pairs) is discovered in this paper. Such error accu-mulation is considered to arise from modality-inconsistent correlations. To address this issue, a novel method termed Geometric-Semantic Learning (GSL) is proposed. Firstly, GSL leverages the Fourier transform to emphasize seman-tic representations and reduce cross-modal inconsistencies caused by perturbations in non-critical fine-grained fea-tures, thereby alleviating the error accumulation problem. After that, a Geometry-Aware Label Correction (GALC) method is introduced to re-estimate soft correspondence la-bels by leveraging angular consistency between noisy sam-ple pairs and anchor pairs across different modalities. Fi-nally, a semantically constrained triplet loss is employed to regulate sample distances using semantic information, enabling robust separation of clean and noisy pairs dur-ing the training process. Extensive experiments on three benchmark datasets demonstrate that GSL consistently out-performs existing methods in retrieval accuracy. 

# 1. Introduction 

Cross-modal matching is a fundamental task in multi-modal learning, aiming to establish associations between different modalities. Most existing approaches assume that the training data pairs are perfectly aligned. However, this assumption does not hold for large-scale, web-crawled datasets such as Conceptual Captions, which often contain a significant number of mismatched pairs commonly referred

to as noisy correspondences [1–3]. In recent years, the challenge of noisy correspondences has attracted increasing attention [4–6]. This line of re-search aims to identify and distinguish noisy correspon-dences, which provide models with more reliable and accu-rate supervision signals, thereby mitigating the negative im-pact of noisy correspondences. However, existing methods [7] remain vulnerable to error accumulation, which primar-ily stems from misclassification during sample partitioning and the inaccurate estimation of soft correspondence labels. To alleviate this issue, several approaches have been pro-posed. For example, fine-grained partitioning strategies [8] attempt to better capture ambiguous sample boundaries and reduce propagation errors. Others incorporate robust loss functions [9–11] to enhance the model’s stability and re-duce the impact of noisy samples during optimization. In addition, recent studies have explored more accurate la-bel correction mechanisms. These methods [12, 13] rely on anchor samples as references, modeling the relationship between noisy samples and their corresponding anchors through cross-modal geometric consistency to enhance the reliability of soft correspondence labels. However, the above methods mainly rely on the consis-tency of the semantic geometric structures across different modalities. The ideal geometric consistency (Fig. 1(a)) leverages high-level semantic associations to correct noisy correspondence labels. But it still suffers from a critical source of error accumulation—anchor correlation discrep-ancy—arising from inconsistent cross-modal anchor pairs. Features extracted from the spatial domain contain not only semantic representations but also a significant amount of lo-cal detail representations. Such local detail feature compo-nents are considered to be the fundamental cause of cross-modal anchor correlation discrepancies ( ∆d). As illus-trated in Fig. 1(b), once anchor correlation discrepancy oc-curs, the consistency of the cross-modal geometric struc-ture is distorted. In addition, the anchor correlation discrepancy was also computed on the Flickr30K with a noise rate of 0.2 and visualized the dis-tribution of samples across different discrepancy intervals using a heatmap (Fig. 1(c)). The results show that anchor correlation discrepancies are widely present among cross-modal samples, with approximately 23% of the samples ex-hibiting a discrepancy greater than 0.3. On the other hand, existing methods typically rely on sample-wise loss values to distinguish clean samples from noisy ones. However, the triplet loss exhibits inconsistent sensitivity to different types of noisy correspondences, making it prone to failure under noisy conditions, which directly reduces the accuracy and reliability of sample partitioning. Finally, we propose a Semantic-Constrained Triplet Loss (SCT) ,which incorporates semantic correlation as an explicit reg-ularization term to guide sample partitioning and model op-timization. Specifically, inspired by [12] and [14], we intro-duce intra-modal semantic consistency and inter-modal se-mantic asymmetry as regularization constraints, encourag-ing the model to focus more on semantic information during updates, and achieving more robust optimization. In summary, the main contributions of this paper can be summarized as follows: (1) This paper provides a detailed analysis of error accu-mulation in noisy correspondence learning and, to the best of our knowledge, first identifies anchor correlation discrep-ancy as a key factor that exacerbates the error accumulation problem. (2) A FoA 2 method is proposed to alleviate the anchor correlation discrepancy caused by local detail feature com-ponents. Meanwhile, a geometry-aware label correction module is proposed to re-estimate the soft correspondence labels. (3) A semantic-constrained triplet loss is designed to guide the model in separating clean and noisy samples more effectively and to further enhance the robustness of the training process. 

# 2. Related Work 

## 2.1. Cross-Modal Matching 

Cross-modal retrieval [15–17] aims to search for rele-vant data across different modalities using a query from one. Existing research in this area primarily falls into three cat-egories. Global-level methods focus on aligning image and text features holistically, leveraging modality-specific en-coders to establish global correspondence [18, 19]. Local-level methods emphasize fine-grained alignment, captur-ing semantic relationships between specific visual regions and textual elements [20, 21]. Region-block-level meth-ods, positioned between global and local approaches, aggre-gate multiple image regions into coherent blocks and align them with textual fragments to learn more structured associ-ations [22, 23]. Despite their differences, the above methods share the same assumption: the training data pairs are per-8665 Figure 2. fectly aligned. Nonetheless, with the increasing reliance on web-crawled datasets, perfect alignment has become pro-hibitively expensive and practically unfeasible. Therefore, ensuring model robustness under noisy correspondence has become a critical challenge. 

## 2.2. Noisy Correspondence Learning 

Noisy correspondence learning aims to mitigate the ad-verse effects of mismatched pairs through carefully de-signed methods. The NCR model [7] was the first to address this problem. It employs a two-component Gaussian Mix-ture Model to distinguish between clean and noisy samples and applies different strategies to correct the soft correspon-dence labels of each category. Such an idea has been widely adopted and extended in recent works addressing noisy cor-respondences. For instance, some research [12, 24] has pro-posed improved soft correspondence label correction tech-niques to enhance the accuracy of soft correspondence la-bels. Other research introduced finer-grained data parti-tioning approaches by leveraging techniques, including un-certainty modeling [25, 26], energy-guided [27], and meta-learning [28]. In addition to sample partitioning methods, some researchers have focused on enhancing the robustness of loss functions to reduce errors arising from the inabil-ity of conventional loss functions to accurately characterize sample relationships, such as evidential learning [26] and complementary learning [29, 30]. However, existing meth-ods share a critical limitation: they assume that anchors are well-aligned, and they ignore the semantic correlation be-tween samples. This is essential for mitigating error accu-mulation. 

# 3. Methodology 

## 3.1. Problem Formulation 

Image-text matching is used as a representative exam-ple to explore the issue of noisy correspondences in cross-modal matching. Given a training set D = ( Ii, T i, y i)Ni=1 ,where (Ii, T i) denotes the i-th image-text pair and yi ∈{0, 1} is the corresponding label indicating whether the pair is matched ( yi = 1 ) or not ( yi = 0 ). The goal of cross-modal matching is to minimize the distance between posi-tive pairs while maximizing the distance between negative pairs. Nevertheless, noisy correspondences occur when a negative pair ( yi = 0 ) is incorrectly annotated as a posi-tive pair ( yi = 1 ). As a result, the model treats truly nega-tive samples as positive during training, leading to degraded performance. Noisy correspondence learning has been proposed to mitigate noisy correspondences, but it still suffers from er-ror accumulation. We argue that anchor correlation discrep-ancy is a key factor causing this issue. The anchor correla-tion discrepancy is defined: 

∆d = |SV − ST | (1) where SV and ST denote the similarities between anchor sample pairs in the image and text modalities, respectively. When ∆d occurs, the cross-modal geometric consistency is distorted (Fig. 1(b)), resulting in biased label correction. As training proceeds, the re-estimated soft correspondence la-bels gradually deviate from the true semantic relationships, leading to error accumulation. To address this issue, a novel GSL is proposed in this paper. As shown in Fig. 2, the proposed model consists of three core components: Fourier-Based Anchor Align-ment, Geometry-Aware Label Correction, and Semantic-Constrained Triplet Loss. The following sections detail their core components. 

## 3.2. Anchor Correlation Discrepancy 

In this section, the cause of anchor correlation discrep-ancy is analyzed from a feature-level perspective. The vi-sual and textual features contain not only global semantic components but also varying amounts of local detail com-ponents. Due to significant differences in the local de-8666 Figure 3. Therefore, label correction for noisy samples is conducted in the frequency-domain feature space to fully exploit its stable structural representations.

## 3.5. Semantic-Constrained Triplet Loss Module 

For noisy correspondence learning, a robust loss func-tion plays a critical role in facilitating model optimization and improving the ability to distinguish between clean and noisy samples. To this end, a Semantic-Constrained Triplet Loss Module (SCT) is proposed, where semantic correla-tion is embedded to regularize the triplet loss for better noise robustness. This loss function imposes constraints on the model through both intra-modal semantic consistency and inter-modal asymmetry, thereby enhancing the model’s robustness under noisy conditions and improving its ability to distinguish between clean and noisy samples.

As illustrated in Fig. 8668 5(a), the core idea is that variations within one modal-ity should correspond proportionally to those in the other modality. This intra-modal semantic consistency can im-pose stronger constraints on clean samples. # 4. Experiments 

## 4.1. Datasets and Evaluation Metrics 

Extensive experiments are conducted on three widely used image-text retrieval datasets. 

Flickr30K [33]: This dataset contains 31,014 images, each annotated with five textual descriptions. In the experimen-tal setup, 1,014 images are used for validation, 1,000 for testing, and the remaining 29,000 for training. 

MS-COCO [34]: This dataset comprises 123,287 images paired with five captions each. For this work, 5,000 images are used for validation, 5,000 for testing, and 113,287 for training. 

Conceptual Captions [35]: This large-scale real-world dataset is characterized by noisy image-text pairs, with an estimated 3%-20% of the pairs being noisy. A subset of 152,000 pairs, referred to as CC152K , is utilized in this study, with 150,000 for training and 1,000 each for testing and validation. 

Evaluation Metrics: Standard evaluation protocols for re-trieval tasks are followed, with Recall at K (R@K) as the primary metric. R@K measures the proportion of relevant items retrieved among the top K results. Reported results in-clude R@1, R@5, and R@10, along with the sum of image-to-text and text-to-image recall scores, denoted as RSum. 

## 4.2. Implementation Details 

For all experiments, the Adam optimizer is adopted with an initial learning rate of 2e − 4. The batch size is set to 128, the word embedding dimension is set to 300, and the dimensionality of the joint embedding space is 2048. The soft margin ˆα is set to 0.2. The balancing factors in the 

LSCT are configured as ζ = 1 .0 and η = 0 .1. We use 

Lw for 10 warmup training epochs, and the total number of training epochs after warmup is 40, during which LSCT is applied.

# 5. Conclusion 

This paper identifies a previously overlooked key factor leading to error accumulation-anchor correlation discrep-ancy. To deal with the above issue, a model named GSL is proposed in this paper. GSL leverages a Fourier-based anchor alignment algorithm to improve correlation consis-tency among anchor samples. The aligned representation is then utilized within a geometry-aware label correction algorithm to estimate reliable soft correspondence labels. Furthermore, a semantic-constrained triplet loss is intro-duced to mitigate error accumulation caused by sample mis-classification. Extensive experiments on three benchmark datasets demonstrate the effectiveness and robustness of the proposed approach. 8671 6. Acknowledgment
